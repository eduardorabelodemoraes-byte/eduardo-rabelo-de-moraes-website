The Choreography of the Crossing

Purpose

This document does not redefine the Crossing.

Its purpose is solely to assign temporal structure to the phenomenology already established by the Threshold documentation.

The physics, perceptual principles, optical material, implementation constraints and phenomenological objectives remain authoritative and unchanged.

Only the duration and cadence of the experience are specified here.

⸻

Total Duration

5.0 seconds

The Crossing must be consciously experienced.

It must never resemble:

* a page loading;
* a UI transition;
* an animation;
* a visual effect.

Instead, it must feel like a gradual relocation of perception from one coherent editorial world to another.

⸻

Temporal Structure

Phase I — Contact

0.00–0.30 s

The visitor touches the Game Localization entry.

The response is immediate.

There is no delay.

Nothing moves yet.

Instead, the visitor immediately feels that the page has become responsive to the act of touching it.

The world acknowledges the gesture.

This instant establishes agency.

⸻

Phase II — Destabilization

0.30–1.20 s

The Home begins to lose the apparent rigidity of ordinary digital matter.

Nothing propagates violently.

Nothing expands explosively.

Nothing ripples like water.

Instead, the editorial surface quietly ceases to behave as something completely solid.

Typography remains readable.

Layout remains recognizable.

Identity remains intact.

Only the certainty of the material begins to dissolve.

The visitor should feel:

“Something fundamental has changed.”

⸻

Phase III — Transformation

1.20–2.80 s

The material enters its richest perceptual state.

This is the Crossing itself.

The page no longer appears to be composed of ordinary pixels.

It behaves as a coherent optical material.

Every region participates.

No single point dominates perception.

The field acts simultaneously.

The transformation reaches its maximum richness without ever compromising editorial legibility.

Nothing is chaotic.

Nothing glitches.

Nothing shakes.

Nothing resembles conventional digital distortion.

The experience should evoke curiosity rather than surprise.

⸻

Phase IV — Passage

2.80–4.20 s

The Home gradually ceases to exist as the dominant perceptual world.

The visitor is no longer observing a transforming page.

The visitor is already crossing into another place.

Attention naturally shifts away from the former world.

The new world quietly establishes itself.

The transition feels inevitable rather than abrupt.

There is no visible cut.

No disappearance.

No replacement.

Only continuity.

⸻

Phase V — Arrival

4.20–5.00 s

The Crossing concludes.

All instability disappears.

The Game Localization world is completely stable.

Nothing residual remains.

The visitor should never wonder whether the transition has finished.

Arrival is absolute.

The Crossing does not fade away.

It resolves.

⸻

Perceptual Rhythm

The Crossing follows a single continuous curve.

It never accelerates violently.

It never pauses.

It never changes direction unexpectedly.

Every instant should feel like the natural continuation of the previous one.

The visitor should never become aware of timing itself.

Only of continuous transformation.

⸻

Emotional Curve

The intended emotional progression is:

Immediate response.

↓

Curiosity.

↓

Recognition that ordinary physical rules no longer apply.

↓

Acceptance.

↓

Crossing.

↓

Arrival.

At no point should fear, urgency or spectacle dominate the experience.

The Crossing is calm.

Deliberate.

Editorial.

⸻

Success Criterion

The visitor should later remember:

“I did not simply click a link.”

Instead, the memory should be:

“I crossed from one editorial world into another.”

If that memory remains after the experience, the choreography has succeeded.
